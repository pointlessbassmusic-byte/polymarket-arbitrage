# Proposal 4: The smallest loop that still learns

*Architect 4 of 4. Lens: frugal minimalism. Written 2026-10-14 against engine v0.1.0 (`mandel/*.py`, `mandel/validation/*.py`; 61 tests pass in 0.9 s), MANDEL.md v0, SYNTHESIS v0, ORIGINAL-IDEAS v0. Every number below is a seed to be replaced by a measured one. Every component names the module it extends. Nothing under `validation/` changes without `actor="human"`.*

## 1. Thesis

Mandel's binding constraint is not reasoning but resolved out-of-sample observations: the gate needs about thirty resolved questions or thirty held-out periods per verdict, and the substrate today holds 0 forecasts, 0 resolutions, 0 proposals and 696 candidate insights that nothing can validate. So the loop is built around a deterministic, zero-token daily job that accrues those observations and writes a small status card, and a model session is started only when the card says something is decidable; most days that means no session at all, and the design's first pre-registered proposal is that its own read-order halves the cost of a session.

Two measured facts underneath this. A fresh session following MANDEL.md's "synthesis first" reads about 19k tokens (MANDEL.md 2.8k, SYNTHESIS.md 14.7k, ORIGINAL-IDEAS.md 1.7k) before doing anything, for knowledge that changes weekly at most; the engine source is another 34k and the knowledge tree 159k. And the bot's only honest out-of-sample stream is prospective: `utils/backtest.py` simulates random mispricings, so the first evaluators must score what was recorded before the outcome was known.

## 2. Components

| # | Component | Role | Extends |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `mandel/daily.py`, `python -m mandel daily` | zero-token observation job | `cli.py`; uses `forecasts.ForecastBook`, `children.ChildRegistry`, `ledger.Ledger`, `PolymarketClient.get_market`, `KalshiClient.get_market` |
| 2 | `state/STATUS.md` | the card: first and often only read | generated by 1 from `state/*` |
| 3 | `state/triggers.json`, `state/declined.jsonl` | start rules; a session runs only on a fired trigger | written by 1; read by 5 |
| 4 | `mandel/PLAYBOOK.md` | per-tier read-order, permitted actions, stop rules | sits beside MANDEL.md; gated like a router file |
| 5 | `mandel/bin/session.sh` + `cycles.py` fields | metered budget accounting | `cycles.Cycle`, `COST_FIELDS`, `CycleLog.efficiency` |
| 6 | `validation/gate.evidence_from_forecasts`, `validation/replay.py`, `proposal-evidence --from-*` | evidence computed by code, never typed | `validation/gate.py`, `forecasts.py`, `cli.py`, `utils/logging_utils.OpportunityLogger` |
| 7 | idea market, minimal | measured proposer track record | `proposals.preregister/decide`, `forecasts.py` |
| 8 | `state/inbox/`, `children.ingest_inbox` | file-only child channel | `children.py` |

**1. `daily.py`.** Eight deterministic steps in order: `verify` and `reconcile`; fetch the watchlist; record resolutions; ingest the inbox; roll up the bot's opportunity log; evaluate triggers; write STATUS; append one `daily.ran` ledger event `{api_calls, wall_seconds, triggers}` and commit `mandel/state`. `state/watchlist.json` holds at most 50 rows `{question_id, question, polymarket_id, kalshi_ticker, resolves_by, added_at}` chosen to resolve two to eight weeks out, because without resolutions in the first month no forecaster proposal can be decided. Each row yields `market:polymarket` and `market:kalshi` forecasts (p = YES mid-price, `market_p` empty so `score_source` finds a real `market-source` reference). Resolution: Polymarket `closed and resolved` with final YES price at or above 0.99 or at or below 0.01; Kalshi `status == "settled"` with `result`; anything ambiguous becomes a human line, not a guess. Ceiling: about 120 API calls, 300 s, zero model tokens. It replaces `ingest && graph` in the OPERATIONS.md timer; those two run only when the hash of `knowledge/` has changed.

**2. `STATUS.md`.** At most 80 lines and 6 KB (about 1.5k tokens), fixed line order: generation stamp; ledger head, verify, reconcile; insight counts; each open proposal with `decidable: yes|no`, the blocking criterion, evidence so far and an ETA from the accrual rate; forecast and resolution counts; the scoreboard for sources with n at least 5 (`n, brier, skill_vs_market, market_reference`); children and unread reports; inbox count; triggers fired today; the budget line (tokens and USD used against caps, sessions by tier, declines); the efficiency line (accepted proposals in four weeks, tokens per accepted proposal, zero-token-day share); one next action; the top backlog item. Every line is a query over state files, so the card cannot collapse like a rewritten context (A3) or carry an unsourced claim (CRITIQUE gap 2). A model never edits it.

**3. Triggers.** Six predicates, evaluated by `daily.py`: T1 `decidable` (a pre-registered proposal whose evidence source now meets its minimum-sample criterion); T2 `child_report` (new inbox file or report row); T3 `resolution_batch` (10 or more new resolutions); T4 `integrity` (verify or reconcile failed: a push to the user, never a session); T5 `weekly_research` (seven days since the last Tier-2 session, `state/backlog.md` non-empty, envelope headroom for a full Tier-2 cap); T6 `human` (`state/inbox/user-*.md`). A trigger blocked by budget is appended to `declined.jsonl` with the reason, so the cost of frugality is itself recorded.

**4. `PLAYBOOK.md`.** Under 150 lines. For each tier: the read-order, the permitted commands, the stop rules, and the two lines of MANDEL.md to re-read (section 2 principles and section 6 refusals, about 1.5k tokens, Tier 2 only). A change to the playbook is a proposal with the metrics of P-A below, because the playbook is the thing that decides what a session costs.

**5. Budget accounting.** `Cycle` gains `tier in {daily, checkin, research}` and `metered: bool`; `COST_FIELDS` gains `usd` and `cache_read_tokens`, validated in `finish` like the existing fields. `session.sh` runs `claude -p "<playbook prompt>" --output-format json --max-turns 30` under a wall-clock timeout, parses `total_cost_usd` and `usage` from the result (if the installed CLI lacks them, it writes the operator estimate with `metered=false`), and calls `cycle-finish`. `state/budget.json` holds the weekly envelope `{week, cap_tokens, cap_usd, used_tokens, used_usd, sessions, declined}`. `CycleLog.efficiency()` gains `tier` and `window_days` arguments.

**6. Evidence from code.** `evidence_from_forecasts(book, candidate_source, incumbent_source, since, n_trials)` returns `{n_resolved, brier_delta, skill_vs_market, out_of_sample: True, n_trials, holdout_id}` from `ForecastBook.scored_pairs`, scoring only questions forecast after the proposal's pre-registration timestamp. `validation/replay.py` re-evaluates recorded dry-run opportunities under an alternative `ArbConfig` (point-in-time by construction, since each row carries its detection timestamp), produces per-day net-edge series at 2x the configured friction for candidate and incumbent, and hands them to the existing `compare_strategies` and `evidence_from_comparison`. Its input is `logs/opportunities.jsonl`: one `json.dumps` of the fields `OpportunityLogger` already formats as text, written beside the text line. `proposal-evidence` gains `--from-forecasts`, `--from-replay` and `--run-tests` (runs pytest, writes `tests_pass`); typed `k=v` evidence remains for `--actor human` only. The gate is unchanged; evidence that was never typed needs no re-running.

**7. Idea market.** `preregister` refuses unless a `mandel:self` forecast exists on `proposal:<id>:passes`, recorded with `market_p` equal to the acceptance base rate so far (0.5 when there is none); `decide` resolves it. About fifteen lines and two hundred tokens per proposal; the only honest measure of whether the proposer improves (ORIGINAL-IDEAS 2, with SYNTHESIS's base-rate and coverage amendments).

**8. Inbox.** A child, or the user on its behalf, commits `state/inbox/<child>-<YYYYMMDD>.md` in a fixed template (`child:`, `summary:`, `learnings:` bullets, `metrics:` pairs, `questions:`, `forecasts:` rows `question_id | question | p`). `children.ingest_inbox()` parses it into `record_report` and `ForecastBook.record(source="child:<name>")`; a parse failure moves the file to `inbox/failed/` and adds a human line. Ingested forecasts take the ingestion timestamp, which is later than the child's own and so scores it against a better-informed market price: the conservative direction, and the card says so.

**Deferred, each with the condition that pays for it.** Helpful/harmful counters on insights: after the tenth decided proposal, since before that they move nothing. `n_scales_agree`: after 100 resolutions, since coarse scales have no n before that. Synthesis regeneration: after ten validated insights. Archive, regions and level specs (proposals 1 and 2): after twenty decided proposals, and only if a rejected parent of an accepted proposal has actually occurred. Vector store: never before three failed retrieval audits, and no audits before the knowledge tree exceeds fifty files. LLM judges: never inside the gate. Bits-per-token allocation: reported in the efficiency line, not obeyed, until its ranking is stable across four weekly windows (its own falsifier in ORIGINAL-IDEAS 4).

## 4. The improvement loop, end to end

06:40 UTC daily: the timer runs `daily`. With no trigger the day ends at zero tokens. With a trigger, `session.sh` starts a Tier-1 check-in with the prompt "Run PLAYBOOK section check-in for the triggers in state/triggers.json". Read-order: `cat state/STATUS.md`; `sed -n` the playbook section for each fired trigger; then only the objects the triggers name (`proposal-show P`, `child-show NAME`, the inbox file). Forbidden at Tier 1: SYNTHESIS.md, `knowledge/`, engine source, `git log`. Permitted actions: compute evidence with `--from-*`, `proposal-decide`, record candidate insights from a report with provenance `child:<name>:report:<id>`, validate or retire insights an accepted proposal names, send guidance built only from validated insights or the failure-mode checklist, append to `backlog.md`. Stop rules: every trigger marked handled; or 25 tool calls (the wrapper enforces `--max-turns 30`); or two consecutive errors on one action; or any action that would touch `validation/`, live settings in `config.yaml`, or MANDEL.md, which is written to the backlog as a draft instead. Always last: `cycle-finish` and commit.

Sundays, if T5 fired: a Tier-2 research session reads the card, the playbook's research section, the top backlog item, MANDEL.md sections 2 and 6, and exactly the files the backlog item names (SYNTHESIS by section, via `grep -n "^## "` then `sed -n`). It produces exactly one of: a drafted and pre-registered proposal with its idea-market forecast; a deterministic evaluator with tests; one knowledge file distilled and ingested. One output, then stop.

A worked cycle. Day 31: `daily` finds P-B has 30 resolved post-registration questions and fires T1. The check-in runs `proposal-evidence P-B --from-forecasts --candidate child:julia --incumbent market:polymarket --since <prereg ts>`, then `proposal-decide P-B`; the gate rejects (skill −0.08). The session records one `fact` insight with provenance `proposal:P-B` ("did not beat the Polymarket price over 30 questions, Oct–Nov 2026"), sends `child-guide julia` with the verdict and one measurement request, and commits. About twelve tool calls, under 40k tokens.

Monthly, by code: `budget.derive` sets each tier's cap to 1.5 times the tier's median metered cost, clipped to between half and twice the current cap, and ledgers the derivation; a human review item is written when a cap moves.

## 5. Validation

`gate.evaluate` and the three standard criteria sets are used unchanged. The new evaluators are the two evidence builders in component 6 and `--run-tests`. Three proposals are pre-registered in the first two weeks:

- **P-A, process (the design validating itself).** Hypothesis: STATUS-first read-order lowers the metered cost of a check-in by half with no loss of triggers handled. Baseline: two check-ins run under MANDEL.md's current read-order (synthesis first), metered. Criteria: `tokens_per_checkin_ratio < 0.5` (median of check-ins 3–12 over median of 1–2), `triggers_handled_ratio >= 1.0`. `n_trials_declared = 1`; `out_of_sample` holds because the measured sessions run after pre-registration. The baseline of two is thin and the proposal says so.
- **P-B, forecaster.** Hypothesis: the first registered child (or, failing one, Mandel's own `mandel` source) beats `market:polymarket` on Brier over its first 30 post-registration resolutions. `forecast_improvement_criteria(min_n=30)`. Expected verdict: reject. The rejection is the first negative result in the store and is worth more than the idea.
- **P-C, strategy.** Hypothesis: `ArbConfig.min_edge = 0.015` beats 0.01 on replayed dry-run opportunities at 2x friction over 30 trading days. `strategy_improvement_criteria()` with `n_trials_declared = 3` (0.012, 0.015, 0.02; all three recorded, best one proposed, deflated for three). Acceptance says nothing about live profitability, since the replay scores detection edge under a declared fill model, not fills; the card prints that sentence beside the verdict.

## 6. Child integration

Everything goes through files in this repository. A child reports by committing an inbox file; it reads guidance with one command at its own session start (`tail -1 mandel/state/children/<name>/guidance.jsonl`); it forecasts by adding rows to its inbox file, and lands on the same scoreboard as the markets. First contact follows MANDEL.md section 5 unchanged but runs as a Tier-2 session, because reading a child's repository is the expensive step: one child per session, 300k-token cap. Remote-session tools, when the user grants them, carry a pointer to the guidance file and nothing else, at most one message per child per week.

## 7. Frugality: the budget, exactly

Units: `tokens` = uncached input + cache-creation + output as metered by the CLI; `cache_read_tokens` recorded but not counted against caps; `usd` from the CLI result. Seeds, to be re-derived monthly by the rule in section 4:

| Tier | Cadence | Token cap | USD cap | Tool/API cap | Wall cap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 daily job | every day | 0 | 0 | 120 API calls | 300 s |
| 1 check-in | only on T1/T2/T3/T6 | 60k | $0.50 | 25 tool calls | 10 min |
| 2 research | at most weekly, on T5 | 300k | $3.00 | 60 tool calls | 60 min |
| weekly envelope | | 800k | $8.00 | | |

So the month costs at most about $35 if every week fires every trigger, and most weeks will not. For comparison: src:12's practitioner ran one scheduled call a day at about $1; the DGM run cost about $22,000 (adj:A1). Three numbers go on the card every day and are the only efficiency metrics: tokens (and USD) per accepted proposal over the trailing four weeks; the share of days with zero model tokens (target at least 60%); the count of declined triggers. Costs remain operator estimates only where the CLI gives no usage, and those rows carry `metered=false`.

What is not done is the deferred list in section 2, plus: no graph rebuild in the daily job, no multi-agent panels inside the loop, no gate re-run of a proposer's backtest (evidence is never typed, so there is nothing to re-run). Each deferred item is a backlog row with its condition, so when the condition is met the work becomes a Tier-2 proposal, not a redesign.

## 8. Fractal fit

Honestly, thin. The one structural instance is the cascade: cheap deterministic observation every day, a bounded check every few days, an expensive act weekly, with each level's budget derived from measured cost at that level rather than copied from the level above (16's "same shape, different decorations"). The scale-consistent edge criterion is deferred until there is enough data for coarse scales to have an n; the parameter-space catalogue is deferred until there are enough decided proposals to form a region. Nothing else here is fractal, and the design says so rather than naming it after a theorem.

## 9. First four weeks

- **Week 1** (one Tier-2 session, ≤ 300k tokens; then zero-token days): build `daily.py` with watchlist, resolutions, triggers and STATUS; write PLAYBOOK.md; wire `session.sh` and the timer; populate the watchlist with 30 questions across both platforms resolving in two to eight weeks; pre-register P-A with its idea-market forecast; run the two baseline check-ins under the old read-order.
- **Week 2** (one Tier-2 session): `evidence_from_forecasts`, `--from-forecasts`, `--run-tests`; the `opportunities.jsonl` line and `replay.py` skeleton; start the bot in dry-run on the Linode; pre-register P-C (three variants declared); inbox template committed.
- **Week 3** (check-ins only if triggered): first resolutions; market sources appear on the scoreboard; if the user introduces a child, first contact as a Tier-2 session and P-B pre-registered against it; first `declined.jsonl` rows, if any.
- **Week 4**: ten check-ins reached if triggers allowed, so P-A becomes decidable and is decided; first monthly budget derivation from metered medians; the four-week efficiency line on the card is the progress report, and no prose report is written.

## 10. Risks

- Resolution rate is slower than the watchlist assumes; thirty resolutions may take six to eight weeks. Mitigation: bias the watchlist to short-dated questions, accepting that those markets are the most efficient; the card's ETA lines make the wait visible instead of inviting busywork.
- The daily job is the one moving part and will rot when an API changes; T4 pushes a failing run to the user, and the job has tests against recorded fixtures.
- Frugality starves learning: a loop that fires rarely accrues little new knowledge. `declined.jsonl` and the weekly research floor make the trade visible and the caps are raised only by measured need.
- Metering may be unavailable in some runtime; costs then fall back to labelled estimates and P-A cannot be decided until metering works.
- Children may not adopt the template; failed parses become human lines rather than silent losses.
- The replay's fill model is optimistic (the bot's dry run fills 80% by default), so an accepted P-C is a detection result, not a trading result.
- The design under-provides for the user's third purpose (models outside host chats). It provides the pipeline: any process that writes rows under its own source name is scored like everyone else. The first non-market model should be a frozen shrinkage of the market price fitted on the first 30 resolutions, a few lines with no model call, to prove the pipeline before anything expensive is built.

## 11. Falsifiable predictions

1. Within two weeks, at least 60% of days spend zero model tokens, and the median check-in costs at most 60k tokens. Wrong if the median exceeds 60k: then the card is not sufficient context and the read-order thesis fails.
2. Within four weeks, at least 30 market forecasts per day are recorded and at least 15 resolutions have accrued. Wrong if resolutions are under 10 at day 28: the watchlist policy is wrong and the forecaster track cannot start.
3. P-A passes: STATUS-first check-ins cost under half the synthesis-first baseline with no drop in triggers handled. Wrong if the ratio is above 0.5.
4. Within six weeks, tokens per accepted proposal is at most 400k. Wrong if no proposal is accepted by week six or the first acceptance costs over a million tokens.
5. No forecaster, child or Mandel, shows `skill_vs_market > 0` at n = 30 in the first eight weeks. If one does, the first action is a leakage check of its timestamps, not a celebration.
6. If T1 fires fewer than twice in four weeks while no trigger was declined, resolution rate was the binding constraint and additional tokens would have bought nothing: the frugal thesis holds. If the backlog grows past five items with triggers declined for budget, the caps are too tight and the monthly derivation must raise them.
