3. **[textbook] Setup vocabulary.** A set in R² is *bounded* if it fits inside some circle and *closed* if it contains its boundary points; two sets are *congruent* if a translation and rotation make them coincide. If T scales the plane by s, T(Q) is a *dilation* of Q when s > 1 and a *contraction* when 0 < s < 1; either way "Q scaled by s". Everything in the section is built from scale, rotate, translate.

4. **[textbook] Definition 10.11.1, self-similar set, and the four examples.** A closed bounded S ⊂ R² is self-similar if S = S₁ ∪ S₂ ∪ … ∪ S_k with the S_i *nonoverlapping* (they may touch at boundaries but share no interior) and each S_i congruent to S scaled by the same factor s, 0 < s < 1. Two numbers summarise the decomposition. Examples: a line segment (k = 2, s = 1/2); a square (k = 4, s = 1/2); the Sierpinski carpet, the unit square with its middle ninth removed recursively (k = 8, s = 1/3); the Sierpinski triangle (k = 3, s = 1/2). Both Sierpinski sets are attributed to Wacław Sierpiński (1882–1969).

5. **[seen] Topological dimension, as the slide puts it.** Left column, verbatim: "Informally, topological dimension is the dimension that we intuitively thinks about. For example, a point is zero dimension, a line is 1 dimension and a square is 2 dimension. * must be an integer." **[textbook]** This paraphrases Remark 2: the dimension of a subspace (Section 4.5) is a special case of *topological dimension* d_T, defined for arbitrary subsets of Rⁿ and always an integer between 0 and n; the book then asserts (Example 5, without proof) d_T = 1 for the segment, 2 for the square, and 1 for both Sierpinski sets, which it treats as curves.

6. **[seen] Hausdorff dimension, as the slide puts it.** Right column, verbatim: "The Hausdorff dimension of a self-similar set S is denoted by dH(S) and is defined by" followed by the typeset formula
   d_H(S) = ln k / ln(1/s)
   and the line "following the definition of self similar". The formula image is a screenshot of the book (Definition 10.11.2), not retyped. **[textbook]** Example 6 computes: segment ln 2/ln 2 = 1; square ln 4/ln 2 = 2; carpet ln 8/ln 3 ≈ 1.8928; triangle ln 3/ln 2 ≈ 1.5850. Equivalently k = (1/s)^{d_H}: the number of pieces grows as (magnification)^{dimension}. **[general]** This is what the wider literature calls the *similarity dimension*; it equals the measure-theoretic Hausdorff dimension only when the pieces do not overlap (the open set condition), which is exactly the nonoverlap clause in Definition 10.11.1. The slide's phrase "following the definition of self similar" is, read strictly, that condition.

7. **[textbook, implied by the slide title "Definition of fractals"] Definition 10.11.3.** A fractal is a subset of a Euclidean space whose Hausdorff dimension and topological dimension are *not equal*. The segment (1 = 1) and the square (2 = 2) are not fractals; the carpet (1.89 ≠ 1) and the triangle (1.58 ≠ 1) are. The slide's two-column layout is this definition's visual form: an integer-valued measure on the left, a real-valued measure on the right, and "fractal" is the name for the cases where they disagree. The definition is Mandelbrot's (1977); the slides and the book do not attribute it.

8. **[seen] The generative turn.** Part 02 announces that linear algebra will be used "to help generate this shape mathematically". The first half of the video describes and measures; the second half constructs. In the textbook this is the move from Definition 10.11.1 (a set *is* a union of k shrunken copies of itself) to the question "which maps produce those copies?".

9. **[textbook] Definition 10.11.4, similitude.** A similitude with scale factor s is
   T(x, y) = s · [cos θ, −sin θ; sin θ, cos θ] · (x, y)ᵀ + (e, f)ᵀ,
   i.e. rotate by θ, scale by s, translate by (e, f): four parameters per map, *contracting* when 0 < s < 1. Examples 7–10 write the maps for the four canonical sets. Segment [0, 1] × {0}: T₁ = ½I, T₂ = ½I + (½, 0). Unit square: four maps, s = ½, θ = 0, translations (0, 0), (½, 0), (0, ½), (½, ½). Carpet: eight maps, s = ⅓, θ = 0, translated to the eight non-central cells of the 3×3 grid. Triangle fitted in the unit square: three maps, s = ½, translations (0, 0), (½, 0), (0, ½). In each case S = T₁(S) ∪ … ∪ T_k(S) exactly. This is the "linear algebra": each piece of the set is the image of the whole set under one small matrix.

10. **[textbook] Theorem 10.11.1 (existence and uniqueness).** If T₁, …, T_k are contracting similitudes with the same scale factor, there is a *unique* nonempty closed bounded set S in the plane with S = T₁(S) ∪ … ∪ T_k(S); if in addition the T_i(S) are nonoverlapping, S is self-similar, so the dimension formula applies. Stated without proof. **[general]** It is Hutchinson's 1981 theorem specialised to equal-ratio similitudes.

11. **[textbook] The deterministic construction (Examples 11–12).** Choose *any* nonempty closed bounded S₀; set S_n = T₁(S_{n−1}) ∪ … ∪ T_k(S_{n−1}). The sequence converges to the S of the theorem. Example 11 builds the carpet from the unit square; Example 12 builds the triangle from an irregular blob and shows S₃ already looking like the triangle. **The attractor depends on the maps, not on the seed.** This is the most likely content of the video's part 02 ("generate this shape").

12. **[textbook] Overlap and the general affine map (Example 13, Definition 10.11.5).** T₁ = ½I and T₂ = ½R(θ) + (0.3, 0.3): for most θ the two half-size images of the unit square overlap. A unique attractor still exists but the set need not be self-similar in the definition's sense and ln 2/ln 2 is *not* a valid dimension for it. The section ends by defining an *affine transformation* T(x, y) = [a, b; c, d](x, y)ᵀ + (e, f)ᵀ with six scalar parameters, of which similitudes are the special case a = d = s cos θ, b = −c = −s sin θ. Shears and unequal scalings are now allowed. Whether the video reaches this slide is unknown.

13. **[general] The theorem in its own terms (Hutchinson 1981).** An IFS is a finite set of contraction mappings {f_i : X → X, i = 1..N} on a complete metric space. The *Hutchinson operator* is F(A) = closure of ⋃_{i=1}^N f_i(A). The IFS has a unique nonempty compact set S with S = F(S), and lim_{n→∞} Fⁿ(A) = S for every nonempty compact A (for contractive systems, every nonempty closed bounded A). The proof is the contraction mapping principle applied to F on the space of nonempty compact sets under the Hausdorff metric: F is itself a contraction there, with the same factor as the worst f_i. John Hutchinson, "Fractals and self similarity", *Indiana Univ. Math. J.* 30(5), 713–747, 1981.

14. **[general] The chaos game (random iteration).** Instead of tracking every image of every point (the deterministic tree grows as kⁿ), iterate one point: choose an index at random each step and apply that map. For the triangle, v_{n+1} = ½(v_n + p_{r_n}) with p₁, p₂, p₃ the corners and r_n uniform on {1, 2, 3}; a recognisable outline appears after about a hundred points. If the starting point is on the attractor every iterate stays on it and, with probability 1, the iterates are dense in it; if it starts elsewhere, each step halves its distance to the attractor and the first few points are discarded. The same random walk on a *square* with four corner maps fills the square uniformly: no fractal, because ln 4/ln 2 = 2 = d_T. Restricting which vertex may follow which (no repeats, no neighbours) makes fractals reappear inside the square. The formula says in advance which case you are in.

15. **[general] Barnsley's fern: four affine maps and four probabilities.** From *Fractals Everywhere* (1988), as given in the Brown MA 242 handout and on Wikipedia:

    | map | a | b | c | d | e | f | p | makes |
    |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
    | T₁ | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0.16 | 0 | 0 | 0.01 | stem (collapses the fern onto a line) |
    | T₂ | 0.85 | 0.04 | −0.04 | 0.85 | 0 | 1.60 | 0.85 | successively smaller leaflets (slight rotation, scale 0.85, shift up) |
    | T₃ | 0.20 | −0.26 | 0.23 | 0.22 | 0 | 1.60 | 0.07 | largest left-hand leaflet |
    | T₄ | −0.15 | 0.28 | 0.26 | 0.24 | 0 | 0.44 | 0.07 | largest right-hand leaflet (includes a reflection) |

    Algorithm: start at (0, 0), draw u ~ U(0, 1), apply T₁ if u < 0.01, T₂ if u < 0.86, T₃ if u < 0.93, else T₄, plot, repeat for 10⁵ steps (the handout's MATLAB loop does exactly this). The probabilities are unequal because most of the picture is produced by T₂ and the stem needs very few points: they are chosen roughly in proportion to the area each map covers. **Changing the probabilities changes the density of points (the invariant measure), not the set they fill (the attractor).** All coefficient magnitudes are below 1, which is the contractivity the theorem needs.

16. **[general] Compression and sensitivity.** 24 coefficients and 4 probabilities, 28 numbers, regenerate an image that would take about a megabyte as pixels; this is the idea behind fractal image compression. Perturbing coefficients produces "mutant" ferns (a published Cyclosorus-like set: T₂ = [0.95, 0.005; −0.005, 0.93] + (−0.002, 0.5), etc.), tilts the plant, or deletes a side of leaflets, so the parameters are informative and the output is sensitive to them.

17. **[general] Collage theorem (Barnsley).** Let L be a nonempty compact set, {w₁..w_N} an IFS with contractivity s = max_i s_i < 1, and h the Hausdorff metric. If h(L, ⋃_n w_n(L)) ≤ ε then h(L, A) ≤ ε/(1 − s), where A is the attractor. Reading: if a target is nearly a union of shrunken copies of itself under some maps, the attractor of those maps is near the target, but the one-step "collage error" is amplified by 1/(1 − s), which blows up as the contraction weakens. The *inverse problem* (given a set, find an IFS whose attractor is it) is solved only in restricted forms (Jacquin's partitioned IFS, 1989) and "remains unsolved" in general.

18. **[general] What makes the attractor exist.** Contractivity is the whole hypothesis. The Algorithm Archive's phrasing: the coefficients being below 1 "is required for an attractor to exist"; Wikipedia adds that practical systems need only be "contractive on average" and that non-contractive IFSs can still have attractors but without the guarantee. Remove the hypothesis and uniqueness, seed-independence and convergence all go with it.

19. **[seen / inferred] Genre.** "Application of Linear Algebra" plus a parenthesised topic is the standard title of a course application project: the COMAP/UMAP module "Using Fractals to Motivate Linear Algebra" (Habecker and Crannell, 2004) has students "adopt" a fractal and work out its affine maps, determinants (areas of image squares), eigenvectors and Hausdorff dimension; a 2018 CUNY City Tech undergraduate poster "Fractals and the Geometry of Matrix Operators" uses the same Anton–Rorres definitions to make Sierpinski sets in MATLAB; University of Utah Math 2270 final projects do the same with Koch and Sierpinski templates. Nabil's video belongs to this genre: a learner re-presenting a textbook section, competently as far as the frames show, with no original claims.

20. **[general] What the video does not do, by construction.** No proofs, no code visible in the frames, no measurement of anything real, no learning. An IFS with fixed coefficients generates unlimited detail and zero new information; "learning" would be the inverse problem of item 17, which is not in the section.

## What Mandel adopts

1. **Define a concept by the disagreement between two independent measurements of it, not by a single number or an adjective.** Because the video's central slide is exactly this: an integer-valued measure (d_T) and a real-valued one (d_H), and "fractal" names the cases where they differ. Mandel's analogue: a strategy is "overfit" when in-sample and walk-forward scores disagree beyond noise; a forecaster is "miscalibrated" when discrimination and calibration disagree; a child's report is "suspect" when its claimed and reproduced metrics disagree. Record both measures and the gap; never average them into one.

2. **A deferred definition is legitimate if it is labelled as deferred and later discharged.** Because the presentation says "it's better to give an informal definition of it for now" and then supplies the formal one two slides later. Mandel keeps the same discipline: an insight may enter the store with an informal statement, but it is tagged `candidate`, its status is visible, and it cannot be used as a gate criterion until the measurable form exists; "for now" must have an expiry.

3. **Keep integer-valued statuses and real-valued scores as different kinds of measure, and do not let one impersonate the other.** Because the slide's footnote "* must be an integer" is the whole point of the contrast: d_T carries one bit of the information that d_H carries as a continuum. Mandel's `status` fields (candidate / validated / retired) are topological: coarse and discrete. Its scores (Brier, log loss, contraction ratio, confidence) are Hausdorff-like: continuous. A promotion rule that thresholds a score into a status must store the score alongside the status, because the status alone cannot be un-rounded.

4. **A description is understood when a short rule regenerates it; "use linear algebra to generate this shape" is the test, not the picture.** Because the video's second half turns from describing the Sierpinski sets to producing them from three or eight small matrices, and the textbook's Example 12 shows the rule regenerating the shape from an arbitrary seed. Mandel stores knowledge as generator plus parameters wherever it can and records the ratio bytes(rule)/bytes(output); an insight that cannot be regenerated from a stated rule is a lookup table.

5. **Two sources that descend from the same canonical text are one source for the purpose of convergence.** Because resource 08 is a re-presentation of the same Anton–Rorres §10.11 that resource 09 lectures from; their agreement on every definition is inheritance, not confirmation. Mandel's `store.py` currently accepts `converge:src:08,src:09` because it only checks that two provenance refs are distinct and well-formed. Every knowledge file therefore declares a lineage root, and convergence requires distinct roots. This is the nonoverlap clause of Definition 10.11.1 applied to evidence: overlapping pieces inflate k without adding information.

6. **Compute what a procedure should produce before running it, and treat a mismatch as the finding.** Because the dimension formula tells you, before any iteration, that four half-scale maps on a square give a plain square (ln 4/ln 2 = 2 = d_T) while three give a fractal triangle; the chaos game then shows a uniform fill versus a Sierpinski triangle, exactly as predicted. Each Mandel loop records a pre-run prediction (expected contraction, expected size of the validated set, expected direction of a metric) and compares it with the run; "as expected" is only allowed when "expected" was written down first. This is the pre-registration rule, restated for loops rather than proposals.

7. **The attractor is set by the maps, not by the seed; test for it.** Because Example 12 builds the triangle from a blob and Hutchinson's theorem guarantees the same limit from every compact seed. Mandel's validated knowledge should be reproducible from its rules regardless of ingestion order; the seed-independence rebuild test specified in resource 09 is kept. (Not independent of 09: same textbook; counts once.)

8. **Convergence is a property of contractions; a loop without a contraction argument runs under a hard cap.** Because the theorem's only hypothesis is 0 < s < 1, and without it uniqueness, convergence and seed independence all fail. Every recurring procedure in Mandel either states the metric in which it contracts, with an empirical estimate of s, or carries `max_iterations`. (Shared with 09; counts once.)

9. **Small one-step errors are amplified by 1/(1 − s); marginal improvements need more evidence, not less.** Because the collage theorem bounds the distance to the fixed point by the one-step error times 1/(1 − s), which diverges as the contraction weakens. A proposal claiming s = 0.95 is asserting something whose fixed point is twenty times as sensitive to evaluation noise as one claiming s = 0.5. (Shared with 09; counts once.)

10. **Every map keeps positive probability; weights shape density, never support.** Because in the chaos game the probabilities change how densely parts of the attractor are visited and a zero-probability map leaves a hole. Mandel's sampler over proposal classes weights by expected gain with a floor p_min > 0 for every class and logs the realised sampling frequencies so the trial count used for deflation is honest. (Shared with 09; counts once.)

## What Mandel holds loosely or rejects

- **Reject citing this video as an authority for any mathematical statement.** It is a student re-presentation of a textbook that itself states theorems without proof; the chain of custody is Hutchinson → Barnsley → Anton–Rorres → Nabil, and Mandel cites the earliest link that actually proves the claim.
- **Reject counting 08 and 09 as two independent confirmations of anything.** Both descend from Anton–Rorres §10.11; agreement between them is inheritance. Until the lineage check exists in code, no insight is promoted to `validated` on `converge:src:08,src:09`.
- **Hold loosely the lecture's "Hausdorff dimension".** It is the similarity dimension of an exactly self-similar set with one ratio and nonoverlapping pieces; the general Hausdorff dimension needs Hausdorff measure and coincides with the formula only under the open set condition. The slide's "following the definition of self similar" is that caveat compressed to five words; do not quote the formula as general.
- **Hold loosely the topological dimensions of the Sierpinski sets (1).** True, but asserted in the section without proof; "stated in a textbook" is not the same evidence tier as "proved or measured", and Mandel's confidence field should say which.
- **Reject the inference "markets are fractal, so IFS or the dimension formula applies to prices".** The formula requires exact self-similarity, one scale factor and nonoverlap; price series are at most statistically self-affine with scale-dependent exponents and heavy noise. Anything "fractal" about a market is an estimated exponent with a confidence interval, a declared scale range and a beaten null, as resources 15 and A4 specify, never a borrowed formula.
- **Reject the IFS as a model of learning or "self-expansion".** Once the maps are fixed, iteration produces detail and no information; learning is the inverse problem (choosing maps from data), which the collage theorem bounds but does not solve and which is unsolved in general. Mandel measures self-improvement as validated information gained, never as volume of output.
- **Hold loosely the claim, common in this genre of student project, that "all fractals come from some iterated function system".** Escape-time sets (Mandelbrot, Julia; resource 16) and random fractals are not IFS attractors; the IFS attractor is a special, exactly self-similar case, and resource 15 is explicit that self-similarity is the exception among fractals.
- **Hold loosely "probability proportional to area" for the chaos game.** It is a rendering heuristic, not a theorem about allocation; Mandel's analogue (sampling proposal classes by expected gain) needs its own validation, and the only hard rule is the positive floor.
- **Hold loosely every item marked [textbook] as something the video says.** The frames establish structure and three slides; the rest is inference from the section the slides copy. If a transcript ever becomes reachable, re-read this file against it first.
- **Hold loosely the 2024-02-13 date.** It is the date a third-party vote-tracking service first created a record for the video, which bounds the upload date from above but is not it.
- **Reject visual plausibility as evidence.** An IFS draws a convincing fern from 28 numbers; a model that reproduces the look of a price chart has predicted nothing. The only admissible evidence is an out-of-sample score against a null.

## Design implications for the RSI predictive substrate

Phrased against the existing modules: `store.py` (Insight, InsightStore, `set_status` with `converge:`), `ingest.py` (section parser, `_provenance_for`, fingerprinting), `proposals.py`, `validation/gate.py`, `ledger.py`, and the `LoopSpec` / `ContractionEvidence` structures already specified in resource 09's implications (this file adds to them rather than restating them).

**Data structures**

- `SourceLineage` (one record per knowledge file, stored in a small table under `mandel/knowledge/` and loaded by `ingest.py` from a `lineage:` header line in each file): `{ref: "src:08", lineage_root: "anton-rorres-ela-10.11", derivation: "re-presentation" | "lecture" | "original" | "survey", derived_from: ["src:09"] or canonical citations, independence_notes}`. This file's record: root `anton-rorres-ela-10.11`, derivation `re-presentation`; resource 09's record carries the same root with derivation `lecture`.
- `Insight.measures: list[{name, value, kind: "count" | "ratio" | "exponent" | "score" | "status"}]` so that an insight asserting a property of a thing can carry the two measurements that define it, and `Insight.disagreement: {measure_a, measure_b, gap, noise_estimate}` for insights whose content *is* a disagreement (overfit, miscalibration, reproduction failure). A single-measure "fractal / self-similar / overfit" claim is incomplete by schema.
- `Insight.definition_status: "informal" | "formal"` with `formal_by: date | proposal id`. An informal insight may be stored, searched and cited in a proposal's rationale; it may not be a gate `Criterion`.
- `LoopSpec.pre_run_prediction: {metric, predicted_value_or_direction, basis}` and `LoopSpec.post_run: {observed, miss}` for every recurring procedure, alongside the `convergence_argument`, `max_iterations` and `seed_sensitivity` fields from 09.
- `Insight.generator: {rule_ref, params, params_bytes}` and `output_bytes` (from 09), with the added rule that an insight whose `generator` is null is tagged `lookup` and capped at confidence 0.5 like a `mandel:self` claim.

**Loops**

- *Lineage audit at ingest.* `ingest_file` reads the `lineage:` header; a file without one is ingested with `lineage_root = ref` (itself) and a warning in the ingest report. A nightly audit lists every pair of sources sharing a root and every insight whose `validated_by` contains a `converge:` whose refs share a root; such insights are demoted to `candidate` with a ledger entry naming the shared root.
- *Deferred-definition expiry.* A weekly pass lists insights with `definition_status == "informal"` older than a registered horizon (suggest 30 days) and either proposes the formal form or retires the insight; "for now" is enforced to mean for now.
- *Predict-then-run.* Every scheduled loop writes `pre_run_prediction` to the ledger before it starts (hash-anchored like a proposal's criteria), then `post_run`; the cycle summary reports `miss` and the loop's running miss rate. A loop that has never missed in many runs is predicting too loosely and its prediction is tightened; one that misses often has a model problem, which is itself a candidate insight.
- *Regenerate-from-rules spot check.* Each cycle samples a few insights that carry a `generator` and re-runs the generator; a mismatch between the regenerated output and the stored output retires the insight (the rule no longer produces the claim) and records why.
- *Seed-independence rebuild and contraction bookkeeping* as specified in 09; this file adds that the rebuild is also run after any change to `ingest.py` or the lineage table, since those change the operator whose fixed point the validated set is supposed to be.

**Validation rules**

- `set_status(..., by="converge:a,b")` requires `lineage_root(a) != lineage_root(b)`; otherwise it raises, with a message naming the shared root. Convergence of three refs of which two share a root counts as two.
- A `Criterion` in `gate.py` may not reference an insight whose `definition_status` is `informal`; the gate reports this as a pre-registration failure, not a measurement failure.
- Any insight or child report that uses the words "fractal", "self-similar", "scale-free" or "overfit" must carry two named measures and their gap, or it stays `candidate` with the tag `single-measure`.
- A loop without a `pre_run_prediction` may run, but its results may not be described as "expected" in a proposal's evidence and do not count toward "as predicted" in cycle summaries.
- Where a status is derived from a score by threshold (candidate → validated on a confidence bound, strategy → adopted on a contraction CI), the score, the threshold and the date are stored with the status; a status with no stored score is treated as informal.
- Pooled confidence across insights is computed over distinct lineage roots only; two insights from one root contribute as one.

**Metrics**

- `lineage_redundancy`: share of `validated` insights whose only validation is a `converge:` with refs sharing a root (target 0; the current value should be computed once as a baseline, since 08 and 09 are already in the store).
- `informal_criteria_count`: number of gate criteria referencing informal insights (target 0).
- `prediction_miss_rate` per loop and overall; also `prediction_tightness` (how often the observed value fell inside the predicted interval), to catch vacuous predictions.
- `two_measure_coverage`: fraction of insights that assert a property (overfit, calibrated, fractal, improved) and carry both defining measures.
- `regeneration_pass_rate`: fraction of sampled generator-bearing insights that regenerate.
- The 09 metrics (`contraction_ratio`, `seed_sensitivity`, `compressibility`, `overlap_fraction`, `exploration_floor_hits`) are kept unchanged.

## Cross-links to other provided resources

- **09 (Asher Roberts, Linear Algebra 10.11 Fractals).** The same textbook section, lectured by a mathematician with the write-along notes published; 09 is the file to read for the mathematics. 08 adds: frame-verified confirmation that this section is the source, the two-measure definition as a presentation's centrepiece, and the lineage lesson (08 and 09 share a root and must not count twice).
- **15 (3Blue1Brown, fractal dimension).** The d_H here is the exact-self-similarity special case of 15's box-counting slope; 15 generalises "must be an integer" versus non-integer to measured roughness over a declared scale range, and states that self-similar fractals are the exception. The two files together separate "self-similarity dimension" (a formula) from "fractal dimension" (an estimate with a confidence interval).
- **16 (Hubbard, structure of the Mandelbrot set).** A counterexample to the genre's "all fractals come from an IFS": the Mandelbrot set is an escape-time set, only quasi-self-similar, with boundary of Hausdorff dimension 2 and topological dimension 1, so it is a fractal by Definition 10.11.3 while being nothing like an IFS attractor.
- **10 (Nipun Batra, seven linear algebra ideas).** Matrices as transformations and composition: a similitude is rotation followed by scaling plus a translation, four numbers; the affine map is six; the attractor is what repeated composition of a few such maps leaves invariant.
- **05 (3Blue1Brown, neural networks).** A network layer is an affine map followed by a nonlinearity; an IFS is a random composition of affine maps with no nonlinearity, and its attractor is the fixed point of applying "the same layer" forever. The contrast shows what the nonlinearity buys (expressivity) and what the contraction buys (a guaranteed fixed point).
- **07 (McHara, linear models to deep networks).** Parameter count as capacity: the similitude's four parameters versus the affine map's six is the smallest instance of the capacity question, and the fern's 28 numbers versus a megabyte of pixels is the compression side of it.
- **11 (Veritasium, Markov chains).** The chaos game is a Markov chain on the attractor (an iterated random map); its stationary distribution is the invariant measure that the probabilities shape, and its support is the attractor that the maps alone determine. "Weights change density, not support" is a Markov-chain statement.
- **04 (Emergent Garden, recursive self-improvement).** An improvement loop is an operator; it has a well-defined limit only if it is a contraction in some metric, and its limit should not depend on the seed. Hutchinson's theorem is the clean mathematical case of what 04 reaches for empirically, and the seed-independence rebuild test is the common test.
- **01 (Seife, Decoding the Universe).** Information and compression: the fern's 28 numbers are a generator, the megabyte of pixels is its output; information is in the generator, and the attractor's infinite detail carries none that the generator did not.
- **06 (Herk, Claude second brain).** Knowledge files that are generators (rules plus parameters that reproduce a conclusion) versus files that are transcripts (lookup tables); the regenerate-from-rules check is the operational difference.
- **12, 13, 14 (AI Pathways, DaviddTech trading pipelines).** The transfer from this material to trading is the measurement discipline only: pre-run prediction, two-measure definitions of "improved" (in-sample versus walk-forward), contraction ratios with intervals, and a flat refusal to import IFS structure into price models by analogy.
- **02, 03 (Mossbridge).** The slide's "informal definition for now" is the posture those resources need and lack: a deferred definition is acceptable when it is discharged by a measurable one; one that is never discharged stays informal, and Mandel's `definition_status` field makes the difference visible.
