## What it says (core ideas)

The video's frame, from its chapters: two years of using Claude for trading reduce to one distinction, *deterministic systems* versus *non-deterministic systems*, followed by how to combine them. [desc] The creator's own one-line statement of the combined approach, from the sibling video: "non-deterministic AI analysis with deterministic automated monitoring." [sibling]

1. **Deterministic system (his usage):** a trading or monitoring system that "runs on fixed rules coded by Claude." Claude writes the code once; the code then runs without the model in the loop, so the same inputs always produce the same outputs. He says this is what he "usually builds": fully automated systems whose rules are explicit. Examples he gives: computing option Greeks and P&L, tracking VIX, market breadth and credit spreads as "deterministic macro scores," and alert predicates on price targets, stops, new strikes and expiries, and implied-volatility moves. [sibling]

2. **Non-deterministic system (his usage):** the model itself doing research and judgment on each call: screening stocks, scoring candidates on catalyst timing and the implied-volatility environment, writing theses, summarising news and sentiment, reading charts. The same prompt can return different output on each run, so this layer is not reproducible in the way code is. He notes that this research was "mostly qualitative, not backtested." [sibling]

3. **Why the split matters (the organising lesson):** the two layers have different failure modes and must be evaluated differently. Deterministic rules can be backtested on history, run cheaply and audited; they cannot read context. The model can read context; it cannot be backtested in the ordinary sense, costs tokens per call, and varies. Mixing them without naming which is which is, on this reading, the main beginner error the channel keeps returning to. [sibling, mine]

4. **Combining them, the pattern he actually ran:** deterministic code gathers facts (spot prices, options chains, snapshots saved to disk, Greeks, P&L, macro scores); a scheduled, single daily model pass interprets those facts through a fixed four-layer prompt chain: (i) data and valuation, (ii) portfolio analytics, (iii) market context and news, (iv) alerts and dashboard; the output is a dashboard plus optional push notifications; the human makes every trade decision. The Claude API is called once a day, at roughly $1/day. Early in the experiment he used the web chat instead of the API to hold costs down. [sibling]

5. **The model as designer, not executor.** In his fully automated systems Claude writes the rules and the model is out of the execution path. In the manual Robinhood experiment Claude acted as "screener and portfolio manager" but he remained the "human decision-maker." In neither case does the model place trades on its own judgment. [sibling]

6. **Prompt specificity.** "Specific, detailed instructions produced better output than generic queries": account size, risk tolerance, constraints, and the exact scoring factors were spelled out before Claude drafted a monthly strategy. [sibling]

7. **Risk rules he states for the options experiment:** aim for large upside with capped downside (long LEAP calls, no short premium); avoid theta decay by holding only options with more than 45 days to expiration; diversify across sectors and catalyst windows; target recently sold-off names with a dated recovery catalyst; pick strikes and expirations to manage leverage; alerts on targets, stops and IV changes. These are stated rules, not tested ones. [sibling]

8. **Dashboard contents (what "monitoring" means concretely):** net asset value, active alerts, per-position theta decay, a strike ladder (bid, ask, IV, volume, open interest), aggregate Greeks, macro indicators (VIX, breadth, credit spreads), and news on holdings. [sibling]

9. **His backtesting procedure, from the 25,000-strategy test:** take a discretionary methodology (ICT "Silver Bullet": liquidity sweep, displacement, fair value gap), convert every judgment call into an explicit adjustable parameter ("dial"); the dials define a configuration space (about 258 million combinations); test a random sample (25,000) plus the popular named variants; data: 16 years of one-minute NQ and ES futures, over 10 million simulated trades; include commissions and slippage; apply "luck corrections and profitability checks"; report survivors. [sibling]

10. **His backtesting results:** about 1.7% of configurations survived; the best win rate was about 52% against the 70–80% commonly claimed; the method's special time windows showed no advantage and sometimes hurt; the named variants "looked decent at first but failed under stricter evaluation"; the one rule that carried the edge was requiring a liquidity sweep; survivors were "simple, frequent signals." [sibling]

11. **What that procedure omits, as far as the summaries show:** no train/test split or hold-out period, no definition of the "luck correction," metrics limited to win rate and survival rate (no expectancy, drawdown, Sharpe, or trades per configuration). [sibling, mine]

12. **A sanity check on the survival rate.** [mine] With 25,000 configurations and a conventional 5% false-positive rate per test, pure noise would produce about 1,250 "survivors" (5%). A 1.7% survival rate (about 425) is *below* the noise expectation, so without knowing the correction method the result is consistent with no edge at all. The creator's conclusion that most of the methodology does not work is therefore the well-supported part; the claim that a residual 1.7% does work is the part that needs the missing hold-out test.

13. **Trade review as a feedback loop.** A companion video is devoted to using Claude to review one's own trades (chapters: system setup, results, architecture, prompts), and the community's "performance tracking" course track is described as data logging, pattern analysis, and feedback loops. The lesson is that the model is most reliably useful reading structured records of what you did, not predicting what the market will do. [sibling, community]

14. **Headline result and its framing.** The sibling video reports a Robinhood account growing from about $66,000 to about $169,000 (about +155%) in May 2026 using long-dated small-cap calls. It is presented with disclaimers (not financial advice, not a guarantee), as a one-month challenge, and with the research explicitly not backtested. [sibling]

15. **The companion strategy video (probably resource 13) builds a regime model, not a signal.** "How To Actually Use Claude Code for Trading Strategies (Like a Quant)" has chapters 00:00 Claude Code Trading Model; 00:57 Hidden Markov Models; 08:15 Building Core Logic; 12:00 Using Claude Code; 19:45 Adjusting Trading Model, and its description says the approach copies "top hedge funds and quants." The relevance here: the deterministic layer in his stack is not only alert predicates but can be a fitted statistical model (an HMM over regimes) whose parameters are themselves a backtestable object. [sibling]

16. **Background the video assumes.** [bg] A trained neural network is a deterministic function; the non-determinism in LLM products enters at sampling (temperature > 0) and, even at temperature 0, from batching and floating-point non-associativity on accelerators, so "deterministic settings" do not fully remove run-to-run variance. Determinism is a property about *reproducibility*, not *validity*: a deterministic rule can be exactly as overfit as a stochastic one. Backtesting is only defined for a decision function that can be replayed on past data; a model that was trained on text through 2025 and is asked to "analyse" 2024 prices cannot give a clean out-of-sample answer, which is the deeper reason his research layer "was not backtested."

### Independent practitioner corroboration on the research hints [bg-other]

These are not AI Pathways and do not cite it. They are the closest written material to what the research hints asked for (prompt structures, backtesting discipline, overfitting traps, data pipelines, journaling, risk rules) and are included so Mandel can see where the creator's reconstructed lessons are echoed by others and where the genre as a whole is silent.

17. **Plan before build; make the model interview you.** Roman Blackwood ("AI in Trading", 900+ hours with Claude Code) says the failure he kept hitting was "The problem isn't the code. There was no plan": data source, timeframe, exact entry definition, exit method (fixed stop, time-based, reversal), and handling of earnings, halts and gaps must be settled before code is written, because they are cheap to fix then and expensive later. Angus Li ("Algo Trading Success", Quantiacs Q22 winner, 1,200+ hours) gives the same rule with OpenCode's Plan mode: describe strategy, asset and date range, let the agent ask about data source, framework and outputs, and fix logic there. [bg-other]

18. **Treat the model as a junior quant; specify the function.** Both authors converge on the same prompt shape as the creator's "specific, detailed instructions": a named function, typed inputs (a DataFrame with date, close, volume), a precise rule (10-day return > 5% and volume > 1.5x its 20-day average), a typed output (boolean signal), and a "nothing else" constraint. Blackwood: "Your job isn't to code. It's to make the instructions tight enough that the AI's guesses are correct ones." Angus Li's example is a 50/200-day SMA crossover on the S&P 500, 100% invested or 100% cash, January 2010 to December 2025, with the human as "senior manager" setting goals, context and constraints and making the final decision. [bg-other]

19. **Persistent project memory in a file the agent reads at session start.** Blackwood keeps a CLAUDE.md holding data format (daily bars, no dividend adjustment), broker setup (paper only, Alpaca), entry and exit rules, risk rules, preferred libraries and dataset quirks; he calls the effect "compound engineering." Angus Li keeps the same content in AGENTS.md. Neither describes a trade journal in the text, though Blackwood's post shows an image captioned "my daily log of statistical tests." [bg-other]

20. **Temperature by role; challenge the first answer.** Angus Li recommends 0.0–0.2 for code analysis and planning, 0.3–0.5 for general development, 0.6–1.0 for brainstorming, and treats the first model answer as a starting point to be interrogated ("what would you improve, what research supports this"). He assigns different models to planning, easy coding, hard coding, documentation and review. [bg-other]

21. **Backtest-to-live attrition and friction.** Bhavishya Goyal (PickMyTrade, a vendor) ran five Claude Code-generated Pine Script strategies on ES 15-minute bars in a $100K paper account from 6 January to 4 April 2026 with 1% risk per trade. Three of five beat SPY (+6.4%); one lost 5.3%. The VWAP mean-reversion strategy's two-year backtest showed a 57% win rate and 2.1R; its 90-day live run showed 41% and 1.4R. He states that backtests on TradingView's defaults (zero commission, zero slippage, perfect fills) are "30–50% too optimistic" and that live Sharpe falls 30–50% below backtest figures under those defaults; his fix is $4–5 per contract per side plus 1–2 ticks slippage, deploy only strategies with positive expectancy after costs, at least 200 backtest trades, and tests across trending, ranging, high- and low-volatility regimes. His post-hoc "+3.1% after adding a 50-EMA filter" to the losing strategy is exactly the in-sample repair Mandel's gate exists to refuse. [bg-other]

22. **What the whole genre leaves out.** [mine] None of these sources, the creator included, describes a hold-out period, walk-forward validation, a multiple-testing correction with a stated trial count, or a proper scoring rule for the model's qualitative calls. The practitioner literature on "trading with Claude" is strong on *how to get working code* and weak on *how to know the code has an edge*. That gap is Mandel's job.

## What Mandel adopts

1. **Every component is typed `deterministic` or `stochastic`, and the type fixes how it may be evaluated.** Because a backtest is only meaningful for a replayable decision function; evidence of the wrong kind (a backtest offered for a model-in-the-loop component, or an anecdote offered for a rule) is rejected by the gate.
2. **The model designs and interprets; code computes and executes.** Because this is the one pattern across all of the creator's systems that survived two years, and it is also the pattern that makes the live-money path auditable.
3. **Stochastic components are scored as forecasters, not backtested.** Because the creator's honest "not backtested" is the right answer for an LLM research layer; Mandel's equivalent is Brier and log loss against the market price on resolved, held-out questions (`mandel.validation.scoring`), with a minimum n before any claim.
4. **Pin and log every model call.** Model id, prompt hash, temperature, seed, input hash, raw output, tokens, dollars, timestamp. Because "non-deterministic" is not an excuse; it is a quantity to measure, and residual variance across k replays is itself a fitness metric for whether that component may sit anywhere near a decision.
5. **A fixed cadence and a dollar budget per interpretation pass.** Because one scheduled call per day at about $1 beat a chat loop on cost and on discipline; Mandel's cycles already log cost, and the cost per validated improvement is a tracked metric.
6. **Monitoring is deterministic predicates in versioned config; the model writes the summary, never the action.** Because alerts on targets, stops and IV are the part of his stack that is reproducible and testable; the narrative layer on top is useful and unverifiable.
7. **Turn discretionary claims into dials, pre-register the trial count, sample the space, then pay for the search.** Because the 258M-configuration space with a 25,000-sample is exactly the multiple-testing setting `deflated_sharpe_ratio` and `expected_max_sharpe` exist for, and because the creator's version lacked the correction that would make its survivors believable.
8. **Report survivors against the chance baseline.** `expected_false_positives = n_trials × alpha`; a sweep whose survivor count is at or below that number is recorded as "no edge found," whatever the best configuration looks like. Because 1.7% of 25,000 is a noise-level result until shown otherwise.
9. **Transaction costs are mandatory, non-null fields in every backtest, and a strategy must survive a friction stress test.** Fees, spread or slippage, and fill probability; for prediction markets, the taker fee schedule and the probability of being filled at the quoted price; then re-run at 2x the assumed friction. Because the creator's inclusion of commissions and slippage is part of why his negative result is credible, and because independent practitioners put default-settings optimism at 30–50% of backtest Sharpe.
10. **Simplicity prior with a trades-per-parameter floor.** Prefer rules with few free parameters and frequent signals; require a registered minimum number of trades per free parameter (a rule of thumb, mine, to be tuned) before a configuration is eligible. Because the survivors in his test were simple and frequent, and because frequent signals give the sample size that makes any statistic meaningful.
11. **Popular win-rate claims enter as hypotheses with the claimed number attached.** Because 70–80% became 52% under test; Mandel records the claim, the test, and the gap as a negative insight so the children do not re-import it.
12. **Specific, bounded instructions to children and to models.** Account constraints, risk tolerance, scoring factors, data sources, and the output schema are stated before any analysis is requested. Because the creator found generic prompts produced generic output, and because a bounded request is the only kind whose answer can be checked.
13. **Interrogate the spec before pre-registration.** A proposal that touches strategy logic must answer a fixed question set (data source and point-in-time availability, timeframe, exact entry, exact exit, edge cases such as halts, gaps, stale quotes and resolution disputes) before `preregister()` will accept it. Because three independent practitioners report that unresolved decisions, not bad code, were their main source of rework, and an unanswered question is where look-ahead hides.
14. **Trade and cycle review runs over structured logs, not memory.** Because the one use of the model the creator trusts most is reviewing records of what was done; Mandel's ledger and cycle log are those records.
15. **Project memory lives in a file read at session start, and that file is itself gated.** Because CLAUDE.md/AGENTS.md-style memory is the practitioner consensus for making a fresh session competent, MANDEL.md already plays that role, and a memory file nobody validates becomes a prompt that quietly rewrites behaviour.
16. **Temperature and model are chosen per role and recorded.** Low temperature for evaluators, scorers and code that will be replayed; higher for hypothesis generation; the setting is a logged field, not a default. Because reproducibility of the stochastic layer is partly a dial Mandel controls, and the record is what lets replay variance be attributed.
17. **A regime-coverage requirement on strategy evidence.** A backtest must report trades and performance in at least trending, ranging, high-volatility and low-volatility buckets (or Mandel's own regime labels), and a proposal with evidence from one regime only is not eligible for adoption. Because post-hoc regime filters are the most common in-sample repair in the genre, and because a `MarkovBaseline` per regime (file 11) is the natural null.
18. **Human decides on live money.** Already principle 13 in MANDEL.md; this resource is an independent practitioner arriving at the same rule after two years.

## What Mandel holds loosely or rejects

- **The headline return (+155% in one month).** One month, long-dated calls on small caps, a strong tape, one account, self-reported, research not backtested. It is an outcome, not evidence of a method; a single such month is well within the dispersion of leveraged option books with no edge. Mandel records it as an anecdote with no insight weight.
- **"Non-deterministic research" as a source of edge.** The creator does not claim to have measured it, and Mandel has no evidence it exists. The research layer is treated as a forecaster with an unknown skill score until scored.
- **The 1.7% survivors.** Rejected as evidence of edge until a hold-out test and the correction method are shown; accepted as evidence that the tested methodology mostly does not work.
- **"Deterministic equals safe."** Determinism buys reproducibility and auditability, not validity. A deterministic rule found by searching 258M configurations is the canonical overfitting hazard.
- **Win rate as a headline metric.** A 52% win rate says nothing without payoff asymmetry, costs and trade count; Mandel ranks on proper scores and deflated risk-adjusted return, never on hit rate. ## Design implications for the RSI predictive substrate

1. **Component registry.** Each registered component carries `kind ∈ {deterministic, stochastic}`, `inputs`, `outputs`, `evaluation_method ∈ {backtest, forecast_scoring, tests}`, `cost_per_call`, `code_hash` (deterministic) or `prompt_hash + model_id + temperature + seed` (stochastic). Gate rule: `evidence.kind` must match `component.evaluation_method`; a backtest attached to a stochastic component is a hard reject with reason `evidence_kind_mismatch`.
2. **Model-call log.** Append-only `llm_calls.jsonl`: `{ts, component, role, model_id, prompt_hash, input_hash, temperature, seed, raw_output, tokens_in, tokens_out, usd}`. Replay protocol: run the same frozen input k=5 times; `agreement_rate` = share of runs producing the same decision-relevant field; a stochastic component with `agreement_rate` below a registered threshold cannot be promoted to any role that feeds an alert or a trade. `role ∈ {hypothesize, specify, implement, evaluate, review}` with a per-role default temperature table in versioned config.
3. **Daily cycle shape.** `ingest (deterministic) → features and alerts (deterministic predicates from versioned config) → one interpretation pass (stochastic, budgeted) → ledger and dashboard → human`. The interpretation pass has a hard token and dollar cap per cycle; exceeding it is logged as a cycle failure, not silently retried.
4. **Spec interrogation before pre-registration.** `ProposalBook.draft()` for any `strategy` or `code_change` proposal requires a filled `spec = {data_source, available_at_rule, timeframe, entry_rule, exit_rule, edge_cases[], cost_model, regimes_covered[]}`; `preregister()` refuses a draft with any `null` spec field and records the refusal reason. The question set is a versioned file so it can itself be improved through the gate.
5. **Parameter-sweep protocol.** `sweep = {dials, grid_size, n_trials_preregistered, sampler_seed, cost_model, splits}`; `walk_forward_splits` with embargo; `deflated_sharpe_ratio` computed with the pre-registered `n_trials` (not the number of configurations that happened to finish, and incremented by every post-hoc repair); report `{n_survivors, expected_false_positives = n_trials × alpha, best_dsr}`; verdict `no_edge_found` when `n_survivors ≤ expected_false_positives` or `best_dsr < 0.95`. Register the sweep's result as a negative insight when it fails.
6. **Cost-model schema and friction stress.** `{fee_taker, fee_maker, spread_or_slippage, fill_probability, latency_ms}` required on every backtest record; `null` in any field fails `code_change_criteria` for strategy proposals. `compare_strategies` is run twice, at 1x and 2x the registered friction; the `strategy_improvement_criteria` verdict is taken from the 2x run.
7. **Regime coverage field.** Every backtest record carries `regime_table = {regime_label: {n_trades, sharpe, max_dd}}` using Mandel's regime labels (volatility and trend buckets, or the `MarkovBaseline` states from file 11); eligibility requires `n_trades ≥ min_n` in at least three regimes and the same sign of edge in a majority of them (ties to ORIGINAL-IDEAS #3, scale-consistent edge).
8. **Simplicity constraints.** Store `n_free_params` per configuration; enforce `n_trades / n_free_params ≥ T` (T registered, start at 30) for eligibility; favour frequent-signal rules because they reach the `min_n` in `forecast_improvement_criteria` sooner.
9. **Claim-testing queue.** Claims from gurus, children or papers enter as `{claim, claimed_metric, source}`; a standing proposal type converts a claim into dials and a sweep; the recorded output is `{claimed_metric, measured_metric, gap}`, which is what the children receive instead of the claim. Seed entries: "ICT Silver Bullet wins 70–80%" (measured 52%, source AI Pathways), "VWAP 2σ mean reversion 57% / 2.1R" (live 41% / 1.4R, source PickMyTrade).
10. **Alert config as code.** Alert predicates (edge thresholds, exposure limits, stale-data checks) live in a versioned file read by deterministic code; the stochastic layer may *propose* a change to that file, which then goes through the ordinary gate like any code change.
11. **Challenge pass as a registered stochastic component.** A second model call with role `review` that must state what it would change and what evidence supports it; its output is logged and scored by whether its objections predicted gate rejections (`review_precision`). Because "challenge the first answer" is only worth its tokens if the challenges turn out to be right.
12. **Child onboarding question set.** At first contact, each child is asked to list its components with `kind` and `evaluation_method`, its cost per cycle, its memory file (CLAUDE.md or equivalent) and the hash of it, and which of its claims have been scored out of sample; unclassified components default to `stochastic` and `unscored`.
13. **Metrics added to Mandel's own scoreboard.** `usd_per_cycle`, `usd_per_validated_improvement`, `stochastic_agreement_rate` per component, `sweeps_run / sweeps_with_edge`, `claims_tested / claims_confirmed`, `spec_refusals` (drafts bounced for missing spec fields), `review_precision`.
