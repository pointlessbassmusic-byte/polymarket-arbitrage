## What it says (core ideas)

The video's frame, from its chapters: two years of using Claude for trading reduce to one distinction, *deterministic systems* versus *non-deterministic systems*, followed by how to combine them. [desc] The creator's own one-line statement of the combined approach, from the sibling video: "non-deterministic AI analysis with deterministic automated monitoring." [sibling]

1. **Deterministic system (his usage):** a trading or monitoring system that "runs on fixed rules coded by Claude." Claude writes the code once; the code then runs without the model in the loop, so the same inputs always produce the same outputs. He says this is what he "usually builds": fully automated systems whose rules are explicit. Examples he gives: computing option Greeks and P&L, tracking VIX, market breadth and credit spreads as "deterministic macro scores," and alert predicates on price targets, stops, new strikes and expiries, and implied-volatility moves. [sibling]

2. **Non-deterministic system (his usage):** the model itself doing research and judgment on each call: screening stocks, scoring candidates on catalyst timing and the implied-volatility environment, writing theses, summarising news and sentiment, reading charts. The same prompt can return different output on each run, so this layer is not reproducible in the way code is. He notes that this research was "mostly qualitative, not backtested." [sibling]

3. **Why the split matters (the organising lesson):** the two layers have different failure modes and must be evaluated differently. Deterministic rules can be backtested on history, run cheaply and audited; they cannot read context. The model can read context; it cannot be backtested in the ordinary sense, costs tokens per call, and varies. Mixing them without naming which is which is, on this reading, the main beginner error the channel keeps returning to. [sibling, mine]

4. **Combining them, the pattern he actually ran:** deterministic code gathers facts (spot prices, options chains, snapshots saved to disk, Greeks, P&L, macro scores); a scheduled, single daily model pass interprets those facts through a fixed four-layer prompt chain: (i) data and valuation, (ii) portfolio analytics, (iii) market context and news, (iv) alerts and dashboard; the output is a dashboard plus optional push notifications; the human makes every trade decision. The Claude API is called once a day, at roughly $1/day. Early in the experiment he used the web chat instead of the API to hold costs down. [sibling]

5. **The model as designer, not executor.** In his fully automated systems Claude writes the rules and the model is out of the execution path. In the manual Robinhood experiment Claude acted as "screener and portfolio manager" but he remained the "human decision-maker." In neither case does the model place trades on its own judgment. [sibling]

6. **Prompt specificity.** "Specific, detailed instructions produced better output than generic queries": account size, risk tolerance, constraints, and the exact scoring factors were spelled out before Claude drafted a monthly strategy. [sibling]

7. **Risk rules he states for the options experiment:** aim for large upside with capped downside (long LEAP calls, no short premium); avoid theta decay by holding only options with more than 45 days to expiration; diversify across sectors and catalyst windows; target recently sold-off names with a dated recovery catalyst; pick strikes and expirations to manage leverage; alerts on targets, stops and IV changes. These are stated rules, not tested ones. [sibling]

8. **Dashboard contents (what "monitoring" means concretely):** net asset value, active alerts, per-position theta decay, a strike ladder (bid, ask, IV, volume, open interest), aggregate Greeks, macro indicators (VIX, breadth, credit spreads), and news on holdings. [sibling]

9. **His backtesting procedure, from the 25,000-strategy test:** take a discretionary methodology (ICT "Silver Bullet": liquidity sweep, displacement, fair value gap), convert every judgment call into an explicit adjustable parameter ("dial"); the dials define a configuration space (about 258 million combinations); test a random sample (25,000) plus the popular named variants; data: 16 years of one-minute NQ and ES futures, over 10 million simulated trades; include commissions and slippage; apply "luck corrections and profitability checks"; report survivors. [sibling]

10. **His backtesting results:** about 1.7% of configurations survived; the best win rate was about 52% against the 70–80% commonly claimed; the method's special time windows showed no advantage and sometimes hurt; the named variants "looked decent at first but failed under stricter evaluation"; the one rule that carried the edge was requiring a liquidity sweep; survivors were "simple, frequent signals." [sibling]

11. **What that procedure omits, as far as the summaries show:** no train/test split or hold-out period, no definition of the "luck correction," metrics limited to win rate and survival rate (no expectancy, drawdown, Sharpe, or trades per configuration). [sibling, mine]

12. **A sanity check on the survival rate.** [mine] With 25,000 configurations and a conventional 5% false-positive rate per test, pure noise would produce about 1,250 "survivors" (5%). A 1.7% survival rate (about 425) is *below* the noise expectation, so without knowing the correction method the result is consistent with no edge at all. The creator's conclusion that most of the methodology does not work is therefore the well-supported part; the claim that a residual 1.7% does work is the part that needs the missing hold-out test.

13. **Trade review as a feedback loop.** A companion video is devoted to using Claude to review one's own trades (chapters: system setup, results, architecture, prompts), and the community's "performance tracking" course track is described as data logging, pattern analysis, and feedback loops. The lesson is that the model is most reliably useful reading structured records of what you did, not predicting what the market will do. [sibling, community]

14. **Headline result and its framing.** The sibling video reports a Robinhood account growing from about $66,000 to about $169,000 (about +155%) in May 2026 using long-dated small-cap calls. It is presented with disclaimers (not financial advice, not a guarantee), as a one-month challenge, and with the research explicitly not backtested. [sibling]

15. **Background the video assumes.** [bg] A trained neural network is a deterministic function; the non-determinism in LLM products enters at sampling (temperature > 0) and, even at temperature 0, from batching and floating-point non-associativity on accelerators, so "deterministic settings" do not fully remove run-to-run variance. Determinism is a property about *reproducibility*, not *validity*: a deterministic rule can be exactly as overfit as a stochastic one. Backtesting is only defined for a decision function that can be replayed on past data; a model that was trained on text through 2025 and is asked to "analyse" 2024 prices cannot give a clean out-of-sample answer, which is the deeper reason his research layer "was not backtested."

## What Mandel adopts

1. **Every component is typed `deterministic` or `stochastic`, and the type fixes how it may be evaluated.** Because a backtest is only meaningful for a replayable decision function; evidence of the wrong kind (a backtest offered for a model-in-the-loop component, or an anecdote offered for a rule) is rejected by the gate.
2. **The model designs and interprets; code computes and executes.** Because this is the one pattern across all of the creator's systems that survived two years, and it is also the pattern that makes the live-money path auditable.
3. **Stochastic components are scored as forecasters, not backtested.** Because the creator's honest "not backtested" is the right answer for an LLM research layer; Mandel's equivalent is Brier and log loss against the market price on resolved, held-out questions (`mandel.validation.scoring`), with a minimum n before any claim.
4. **Pin and log every model call.** Model id, prompt hash, temperature, seed, input hash, raw output, tokens, dollars, timestamp. Because "non-deterministic" is not an excuse; it is a quantity to measure, and residual variance across k replays is itself a fitness metric for whether that component may sit anywhere near a decision.
5. **A fixed cadence and a dollar budget per interpretation pass.** Because one scheduled call per day at about $1 beat a chat loop on cost and on discipline; Mandel's cycles already log cost, and the cost per validated improvement is a tracked metric.
6. **Monitoring is deterministic predicates in versioned config; the model writes the summary, never the action.** Because alerts on targets, stops and IV are the part of his stack that is reproducible and testable; the narrative layer on top is useful and unverifiable.
7. **Turn discretionary claims into dials, pre-register the trial count, sample the space, then pay for the search.** Because the 258M-configuration space with a 25,000-sample is exactly the multiple-testing setting `deflated_sharpe_ratio` and `expected_max_sharpe` exist for, and because the creator's version lacked the correction that would make its survivors believable.
8. **Report survivors against the chance baseline.** `expected_false_positives = n_trials × alpha`; a sweep whose survivor count is at or below that number is recorded as "no edge found," whatever the best configuration looks like. Because 1.7% of 25,000 is a noise-level result until shown otherwise.
9. **Transaction costs are mandatory, non-null fields in every backtest.** Fees, spread or slippage, and fill probability; for prediction markets, the taker fee schedule and the probability of being filled at the quoted price. Because the creator's inclusion of commissions and slippage is part of why his negative result is credible.
10. **Simplicity prior with a trades-per-parameter floor.** Prefer rules with few free parameters and frequent signals; require a registered minimum number of trades per free parameter (a rule of thumb, mine, to be tuned) before a configuration is eligible. Because the survivors in his test were simple and frequent, and because frequent signals give the sample size that makes any statistic meaningful.
11. **Popular win-rate claims enter as hypotheses with the claimed number attached.** Because 70–80% became 52% under test; Mandel records the claim, the test, and the gap as a negative insight so the children do not re-import it.
12. **Specific, bounded instructions to children and to models.** Account constraints, risk tolerance, scoring factors, data sources, and the output schema are stated before any analysis is requested. Because the creator found generic prompts produced generic output, and because a bounded request is the only kind whose answer can be checked.
13. **Trade and cycle review runs over structured logs, not memory.** Because the one use of the model the creator trusts most is reviewing records of what was done; Mandel's ledger and cycle log are those records.
14. **Human decides on live money.** Already principle 13 in MANDEL.md; this resource is an independent practitioner arriving at the same rule after two years.

## Design implications for the RSI predictive substrate

1. **Component registry.** Each registered component carries `kind ∈ {deterministic, stochastic}`, `inputs`, `outputs`, `evaluation_method ∈ {backtest, forecast_scoring, tests}`, `cost_per_call`, `code_hash` (deterministic) or `prompt_hash + model_id + temperature + seed` (stochastic). Gate rule: `evidence.kind` must match `component.evaluation_method`; a backtest attached to a stochastic component is a hard reject with reason `evidence_kind_mismatch`.
2. **Model-call log.** Append-only `llm_calls.jsonl`: `{ts, component, model_id, prompt_hash, input_hash, temperature, seed, raw_output, tokens_in, tokens_out, usd}`. Replay protocol: run the same frozen input k=5 times; `agreement_rate` = share of runs producing the same decision-relevant field; a stochastic component with `agreement_rate` below a registered threshold cannot be promoted to any role that feeds an alert or a trade.
3. **Daily cycle shape.** `ingest (deterministic) → features and alerts (deterministic predicates from versioned config) → one interpretation pass (stochastic, budgeted) → ledger and dashboard → human`. The interpretation pass has a hard token and dollar cap per cycle; exceeding it is logged as a cycle failure, not silently retried.
4. **Parameter-sweep protocol.** `sweep = {dials, grid_size, n_trials_preregistered, sampler_seed, cost_model, splits}`; `walk_forward_splits` with embargo; `deflated_sharpe_ratio` computed with the pre-registered `n_trials` (not the number of configurations that happened to finish); report `{n_survivors, expected_false_positives = n_trials × alpha, best_dsr}`; verdict `no_edge_found` when `n_survivors ≤ expected_false_positives` or `best_dsr < 0.95`. Register the sweep's result as a negative insight when it fails.
5. **Cost-model schema.** `{fee_taker, fee_maker, spread_or_slippage, fill_probability, latency_ms}` required on every backtest record; `null` in any field fails `code_change_criteria` for strategy proposals.
6. **Simplicity constraints.** Store `n_free_params` per configuration; enforce `n_trades / n_free_params ≥ T` (T registered, start at 30) for eligibility; favour frequent-signal rules because they reach the `min_n` in `forecast_improvement_criteria` sooner.
7. **Claim-testing queue.** Claims from gurus, children or papers enter as `{claim, claimed_metric, source}`; a standing proposal type converts a claim into dials and a sweep; the recorded output is `{claimed_metric, measured_metric, gap}`, which is what the children receive instead of the claim.
8. **Alert config as code.** Alert predicates (edge thresholds, exposure limits, stale-data checks) live in a versioned file read by deterministic code; the stochastic layer may *propose* a change to that file, which then goes through the ordinary gate like any code change.
9. **Child onboarding question set.** At first contact, each child is asked to list its components with `kind` and `evaluation_method`, its cost per cycle, and which of its claims have been scored out of sample; unclassified components default to `stochastic` and `unscored`.
10. **Metrics added to Mandel's own scoreboard.** `usd_per_cycle`, `usd_per_validated_improvement`, `stochastic_agreement_rate` per component, `sweeps_run / sweeps_with_edge`, and `claims_tested / claims_confirmed`.
