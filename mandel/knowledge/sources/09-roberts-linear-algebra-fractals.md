## What it says (core ideas)

The section's programme, in the textbook's own words (visible behind the notes), is to use "certain classes of linear transformations to describe and generate intricate sets in the Euclidean plane", and to show that this "linear algebra approach also leads to algorithms that can be exploited to draw fractals on a computer". The lecture is a definition → example → theorem → algorithm sequence with no proofs.

1. **Setup vocabulary (Remark 1).** A set in R² is *bounded* if it fits inside some circle, *closed* if it contains its boundary points, and two sets are *congruent* if a translation plus rotation makes them coincide. If T is the linear operator "scale by s" (Table 7 of Section 4.9 in the book), T(Q) is a *dilation* of Q when s > 1 and a *contraction* when 0 < s < 1; either way "Q scaled by s". Everything that follows is built from scaling, rotating and translating.

2. **Definition 10.11.1 (self-similar set).** A closed, bounded S ⊂ R² is self-similar if S = S₁ ∪ S₂ ∪ ... ∪ S_k where the S_i are *nonoverlapping* (they may touch along boundaries but share no interior area) and each S_i is congruent to S scaled by the same factor s, 0 < s < 1. Two numbers, k and s, summarise the decomposition.

3. **Examples 1–4 (the four canonical sets).** Line segment: k = 2, s = 1/2. Square: k = 4, s = 1/2. Sierpinski carpet (unit square with the middle ninth removed, recursively): k = 8, s = 1/3. Sierpinski triangle: k = 3, s = 1/2. The carpet and triangle are attributed to Wacław Sierpiński (1882–1969).

4. **Remark 2 and Example 5 (topological dimension).** The dimension of a subspace (Section 4.5) is a special case of *topological dimension* d_T, defined for arbitrary subsets of Rⁿ. Informally: a point has d_T = 0, a curve d_T = 1, a region d_T = 2, and d_T is an integer between 0 and n. For the four examples: segment 1, square 2, carpet 1, triangle 1 (the book asserts, without proof, that the two Sierpinski sets are topologically curves).

5. **Definition 10.11.2 (Hausdorff dimension of a self-similar set).**
   d_H(S) = ln k / ln(1/s).
   Equivalently k = (1/s)^{d_H}: the number of pieces grows as (magnification)^{dimension}. Example 6: segment ln 2/ln 2 = 1; square ln 4/ln 2 = 2; carpet ln 8/ln 3 ≈ 1.8928; triangle ln 3/ln 2 ≈ 1.5850. **[general]** This quantity is what the wider literature calls the *similarity dimension*; it coincides with the measure-theoretic Hausdorff dimension when the pieces do not overlap (the open set condition), which is exactly the nonoverlap clause in Definition 10.11.1.

6. **Definition 10.11.3 (fractal).** A fractal is a subset of a Euclidean space whose Hausdorff dimension and topological dimension are *not equal*. The segment (1 = 1) and square (2 = 2) are not fractals; the carpet (1.89 ≠ 1) and triangle (1.58 ≠ 1) are. The definition is Mandelbrot's (1977); the notes do not attribute it. The practical content: "fractal" is a statement about the disagreement between two ways of measuring size.

7. **Definition 10.11.4 (similitude).** A similitude with scale factor s is the map
   T(x, y) = s · [cos θ, −sin θ; sin θ, cos θ] · (x, y)ᵀ + (e, f)ᵀ,
   i.e. rotate by θ, scale by s, translate by (e, f). Four parameters per map. It is *contracting* when 0 < s < 1. This is where the linear algebra enters: each piece of a self-similar set is the image of the whole set under one similitude.

8. **Examples 7–10 (writing the similitudes explicitly).** Segment [0,1]×{0}: T₁ = ½I, T₂ = ½I + (½, 0). Unit square: four maps with s = ½, θ = 0 and translations (0,0), (½,0), (0,½), (½,½). Sierpinski carpet: eight maps with s = ⅓, θ = 0, translated to the eight non-central cells of the 3×3 grid. Sierpinski triangle (fitted in the unit square): three maps with s = ½, translations (0,0), (½,0), (0,½). In each case S = T₁(S) ∪ ... ∪ T_k(S) exactly.

9. **Theorem 10.11.1 (existence and uniqueness).** If T₁, ..., T_k are contracting similitudes with the same scale factor, there is a *unique* nonempty closed bounded set S in the plane with S = T₁(S) ∪ T₂(S) ∪ ... ∪ T_k(S). If, in addition, the images T_i(S) are nonoverlapping, S is self-similar (so the dimension formula applies). Stated without proof. **[general]** This is Hutchinson's 1981 theorem specialised to equal-ratio similitudes; the proof is the contraction mapping theorem applied to the operator T(Q) = ∪ T_i(Q) on the complete metric space of nonempty compact sets under the Hausdorff metric. The set S is the *attractor* of the iterated function system {T_i}.

10. **Algorithm 1 (deterministic construction).** Step 0: choose an *arbitrary* nonempty closed bounded set S₀. Step n: S_n = T(S_{n−1}) = ∪_i T_i(S_{n−1}). The sequence S₀, S₁, S₂, ... converges to the S of the theorem. Example 11 builds the carpet starting from the unit square (S₁ is the square with its middle ninth removed, S₂ removes the middle ninths of the remaining eight, and so on). Example 12 builds the Sierpinski triangle starting from an irregular blob S₀, and shows S₃ already looks like the triangle: **the attractor depends only on the maps, not on the seed**.

11. **Example 13 (overlap).** T₁ = ½I and T₂ = ½R(θ) + (0.3, 0.3). For various θ the two half-size images of the unit square overlap. The theorem still guarantees a unique attractor, but the images are not nonoverlapping, so the set need not be self-similar in the definition's sense and ln 2/ln 2 = 1 is *not* a valid dimension for it. Lesson: the dimension formula is conditional on nonoverlap; overlap double-counts.

12. **Definition 10.11.5 (affine transformation).** T(x, y) = [a, b; c, d](x, y)ᵀ + (e, f)ᵀ with six scalar parameters. Similitudes are the special case a = d = s cos θ, b = −c = −s sin θ. Roberts's notes end with this definition. **[textbook continuation]** The section goes on to say that Theorem 10.11.1 extends to contracting affine maps (shears and unequal scalings allowed), introduces a "Monte Carlo approach" to generating the attractor, and shows more general fractals of the Barnsley-fern type; the book's index lists both "Monte Carlo approach" and "self-similar sets" under Fractals.

13. **[textbook continuation / general] The Monte Carlo ("chaos game") algorithm.** Pick a seed point x₀; at each step choose an index σ_n at random from {1..k} and set x_{n+1} = T_{σn}(x_n); discard the first few dozen points and plot the rest. The plotted points lie on the attractor because each map is a contraction toward it. For symmetric sets equal probabilities work; for sets like the fern the probabilities must be nonuniform, roughly proportional to the area of each piece, or some parts are visited too rarely to show. The probabilities change the *density* of points on the attractor (the invariant measure), not the attractor itself.

14. **[general] Barnsley's fern and compression.** Four affine maps (24 coefficients) plus four probabilities, 28 numbers in all, regenerate a fern image that would take about a megabyte to store as pixels. One map ("the terminator") squashes the whole fern onto its stem, one makes the main cascade (about 90% of the picture), two make the lower left and right fronds. Barnsley's *Fractals Everywhere* (1988) popularised the method; the 1986 PNAS paper posed the inverse problem (find an IFS whose attractor approximates a given set).

15. **[general] Collage theorem.** If L is a compact target and h(L, ∪ T_i(L)) ≤ ε in the Hausdorff metric, then h(L, A) ≤ ε/(1 − s), where A is the attractor and s the contraction factor. The error of the fixed point is bounded by the one-step "collage error" times an amplification factor 1/(1 − s) that blows up as s → 1.

16. **Form of the lecture.** Write-along notes (students copy as he writes), textbook figures reproduced, each example worked, no proofs, no code. Roughly six pages of notes. The video sits between Computed Tomography (10.10) and Chaos (10.12, Arnold's cat map, dense orbits, the definition of a chaotic map), which is the book's natural next step from "iterate an affine map" to "iterate a map mod 1 and get unpredictability".

## What Mandel adopts

1. **The attractor is a property of the maps, not of the seed (Example 12).** Mandel's validated knowledge set should be reproducible from its *rules* (ingestion, validation gate, scoring) regardless of the order in which children's reports or sources arrived, because a knowledge state that depends on arrival order is an artefact of history, not of evidence. Test it: rebuild from a permuted seed and compare.
2. **Convergence is guaranteed only for contractions (Theorem 10.11.1).** Every iterative loop in Mandel (re-validation, proposal → evaluate → accept, children's feedback) must either come with a convergence argument or be run under a hard iteration cap and an empirical contraction check, because fixed-point iteration without contraction has no uniqueness or convergence guarantee and can wander.
3. **Measure the contraction factor of each improvement.** For an accepted proposal record s = err_after/err_before on held-out data; s < 1 with a confidence interval is the honest definition of "improved", because the whole theory of the section rests on s < 1, and "error went down on the training window" is not that.
4. **Error amplification 1/(1 − s) (collage theorem).** When improvements are marginal (s near 1), small evaluation noise is amplified into large uncertainty about where the loop is converging, so the gate must demand more evidence (larger n, more walk-forward folds) for small claimed gains than for large ones, because the bound says the fixed point can be ε/(1 − s) away from the target even when each step looks ε-close.
5. **Compression as a quality signal (28 numbers regenerate a fern).** Store knowledge as generative rules plus parameters wherever possible, and track bytes(rules)/bytes(output); an insight that cannot be regenerated from a short rule is a lookup table, not understanding. This is the operational form of the Kolmogorov/Solomonoff names already in `children.py`.
6. **Nonoverlap before counting (Definition 10.11.1, Example 13).** The dimension formula fails when pieces overlap; likewise evidence pooled from overlapping sources (two children citing the same backtest, two articles quoting one study) must be deduplicated before it counts toward confidence, because overlap inflates k without adding information.
7. **Two measures disagreeing is itself information (Definition 10.11.3).** "Fractal" is defined by d_H ≠ d_T. Mandel should treat disagreement between metrics (Brier vs log loss, calibration vs discrimination, Sharpe vs drawdown) as a diagnostic to record, not an inconsistency to average away.
8. **Exploration with positive probability everywhere (chaos game).** Random sampling of which proposal class to evaluate next converges on the same validated set as exhaustive search only if every class keeps positive probability; weight classes by expected "area" (expected gain) but never to zero, because a map with probability zero leaves a hole in the attractor.
9. **Algorithms come after existence theorems.** The section proves (states) that the object exists and is unique before giving two ways to compute it. Mandel should write the acceptance criterion (what "the improved system" is) before writing the procedure that searches for it; this is already the pre-registration rule in `proposals.py`, and the section is a clean mathematical instance of why.

## What Mandel holds loosely or rejects

- **Reject** the inference "markets are fractal, therefore the dimension formula or IFS models apply to prices". The formula d_H = ln k/ln(1/s) requires exact self-similarity with one scale factor and nonoverlapping pieces; price series are at best statistically self-affine with scale-dependent exponents and heavy noise. Anything "fractal" about markets must be an estimated scaling exponent with a confidence interval and an out-of-sample check (see resources 11–14), not a borrowed formula.
- **Reject** the user's framing of an IFS as an "infinitely self-expanding, learning" model. An IFS attractor has infinite detail and zero new information: once the 28 numbers are fixed, iteration produces complexity, not knowledge. Learning is the *inverse* problem (choosing the maps from data), which Wikipedia correctly describes as unsolved in general and which the collage theorem only bounds. Mandel's "self-expansion" must be measured as validated information gained, never as volume of output generated.
- **Hold loosely** "Hausdorff dimension" as defined in the lecture. It is the similarity dimension; the general Hausdorff dimension is defined via Hausdorff measure and only coincides with it under the open set condition. Do not quote the lecture's definition as the general one.
- **Hold loosely** the convergence analogy for RSI. Theorem 10.11.1 needs contractions on a complete metric space. A self-improvement loop operating on code and files has no natural metric in which it is a contraction; the analogy motivates measuring s, it does not license assuming convergence.
- **Hold loosely** the claim that topological dimension of the Sierpinski sets is 1. It is true, but asserted without proof in the section; Mandel should not treat "stated in a textbook" as the same evidence tier as "proved or measured".
- **Reject** estimating fractal dimension from small samples as a trading feature without a null. Box-counting or Hurst estimates on finite windows are biased and noisy; a fractional-Brownian-motion null (graph dimension D = 2 − H, so D = 1.5 for a memoryless random walk) must be beaten out of sample before any such estimate counts as signal.
- **Hold loosely** the chaos-game rule of thumb "probability proportional to area". It is a heuristic for rendering density, not a theorem about optimal allocation; Mandel's analogue (sampling proposals by expected gain) needs its own validation.

## Design implications for the RSI predictive substrate

Phrased against the existing `mandel/` modules: `proposals.py` (Proposal, Criterion, `n_trials_declared`, lifecycle), `validation/gate.py` (`evaluate`, criteria builders), `validation/backtest_eval.py` (`walk_forward_splits`, `deflated_sharpe_ratio`, `block_bootstrap_pvalue`), `validation/scoring.py` (Brier, log loss, ECE), `store.py` (Insight, InsightStore), `children.py` (Child, reports), `ledger.py`.

**Data structures**
- `ContractionEvidence` (added to the `evidence` dict a proposal records before `decide`): `{metric, err_before, err_after, s: err_after/err_before, s_ci: [lo, hi], holdout_id, n_obs}`. The gate reads `s_ci[1] < 1`, not `s < 1`.
- `LoopSpec` (one per recurring procedure in Mandel, stored under `mandel/knowledge/` and referenced from the ledger): `{name, state_space, metric, convergence_argument: "proof"|"empirical"|"none", contraction_estimate, max_iterations, seed_sensitivity}`. A loop with `convergence_argument == "none"` may run only with `max_iterations` set.
- `Insight` (in `store.py`) gains `generator: {rule_ref, params, params_bytes}` and `output_bytes`, so `compressibility = params_bytes / output_bytes` can be computed; and `overlap_group: str` so insights derived from the same underlying evidence are counted once.
- `ScalingEstimate` (candidate insight type): `{series_id, estimator: "box_count"|"hurst_rs"|"dfa"|"variance_ratio", scale_range: [min, max], exponent, ci, null_exponent, oos_window, oos_exponent}`. Never promoted to validated unless `oos_exponent` lies within `ci` and `ci` excludes `null_exponent`.
- `Child.metrics` (in `children.py`) gains `reported_overlap_groups: list[str]` so Mandel can detect two children reporting the same evidence.

**Loops**
- *Seed-independence rebuild* (periodic): replay the ledger's candidate insights into a fresh `InsightStore` in two or more permuted orders and compare the resulting validated sets; store `seed_sensitivity = 1 − Jaccard(set_a, set_b)`. If it exceeds a registered threshold, the validation operator is order-dependent and the loop is flagged in its `LoopSpec` (this is Example 12 as a regression test).
- *Contraction bookkeeping*: every `decide(passed=True)` requires `ContractionEvidence`; Mandel keeps a running geometric mean of accepted `s` values per target (strategy, forecast source, code module) as its convergence-rate estimate. A target whose recent `s` values drift toward 1 is "stalled"; one with any `s > 1` on held-out data triggers `roll_back`.
- *Chaos-game proposal sampler*: when choosing which proposal class to evaluate next, sample with probability p_i ∝ expected gain of class i, floored at p_min > 0 for every class (exploration floor). Log the sampling probabilities in the ledger so that `n_trials_declared` and the realised trial counts can be reconciled for the Deflated Sharpe Ratio.
- *Collage check for compositional models*: before adopting any model built as "a union of rescaled copies of the same component" (multi-timescale strategies, hierarchical ensembles), compute the one-step collage error ε (how well the union of components reproduces the held-out data) and the contraction factor s, and report the bound ε/(1 − s) alongside the headline metric.

**Validation rules**
- `code_change_criteria()` and `strategy_improvement_criteria()` in `gate.py` gain a criterion `contraction_ci_upper < 1` fed from `ContractionEvidence`; a proposal without it cannot leave `evaluating`.
- Minimum evidence scales with marginality: for a claimed s in [0.9, 1), require at least twice the walk-forward folds and the full `block_bootstrap_pvalue` test; small gains get more scrutiny, not less (the 1/(1 − s) amplification).
- A proposal that introduces a new loop must attach a `LoopSpec`; `convergence_argument == "none"` without `max_iterations` is a gate failure.
- Scaling-exponent insights require: estimation over at least two decades of scale, a bootstrap CI, a stated null (fBm with H = 0.5, i.e. graph dimension 1.5, for memoryless series), and an out-of-sample reproduction. Otherwise they stay `candidate`.
- Pooled confidence across insights is computed over distinct `overlap_group`s only (nonoverlap before counting).
- Any claim phrased as "the system is fractal / self-similar" must name the two measures that disagree and report both; a single number is not a fractal claim.

**Metrics**
- `contraction_ratio` (per accepted proposal) and its running geometric mean per target; `stall_count` (proposals with s in [0.95, 1)).
- `seed_sensitivity` (Jaccard distance between validated sets rebuilt from permuted seeds); target near 0.
- `compressibility` per insight and its distribution across the store; a rising median is a sign of understanding, a falling one of memorisation.
- `overlap_fraction` of evidence (share of insights sharing an `overlap_group` with another).
- `exploration_floor_hits` (how often the sampler fell back to p_min), to detect classes being starved.
- For time series: estimated scaling exponent with CI vs null, by series and by window, kept as diagnostics, never as features until validated.
