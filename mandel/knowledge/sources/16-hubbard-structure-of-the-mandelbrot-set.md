## What it says

- **One map, two pictures.** The quadratic family f_c(z) = z² + c, iterated from z. For a fixed c, the **Julia set** J_c is the boundary between points whose orbits escape to infinity and those that stay bounded; the filled Julia set K_c is the set of bounded orbits. The **Mandelbrot set** M is the set of parameters c for which the orbit of the critical point 0 stays bounded, equivalently for which J_c is connected.
- **Parameter space as a catalogue.** M lives in the c-plane, not the z-plane. Each point of M indexes a whole dynamical system; nearby parameters give qualitatively similar Julia sets, and the shape of M near c reflects the shape of J_c (Tan Lei's similarity theorem). M is a map of all the behaviours of the family, which is why it is more than a pretty picture.
- **Connectedness (Douady–Hubbard 1982).** M is connected. The proof constructs a conformal isomorphism Φ_M from the complement of M to the complement of the closed unit disc, using the Böttcher coordinate of each f_c at the critical value; the Green's function G_M(c) = log|Φ_M(c)| and its level curves (equipotentials) and gradient lines (**external rays** R_M(θ), θ ∈ R/Z) organise the outside of M.
- **Combinatorics via rays.** Rays with rational angle land on M; rays whose angles are periodic under doubling land at roots of hyperbolic components; the landing pattern encodes the combinatorics of the dynamics (which periodic orbits exist, in what order). The angles 1/3 and 2/3 land at the root of the period-2 bulb; angles p/(2^n − 1) relate to period-n components.
- **Hyperbolic components.** Open regions of M where f_c has an attracting cycle of some period n. Each is parametrised by the multiplier of the attracting cycle (a conformal map onto the unit disc); the main cardioid is period 1, the disc centred at −1 is period 2, and bulbs attach at points where the multiplier is a root of unity, with internal angle p/q giving a q-fold bulb. The conjecture that hyperbolic parameters are dense in M (hyperbolicity conjecture) is open.
- **Renormalisation and baby Mandelbrot sets.** Small copies of M appear everywhere in M (Douady–Hubbard polynomial-like maps). Near such a copy, a suitable iterate f_c^n restricted to a small domain behaves like a quadratic polynomial again; the copy is the parameter space of that renormalised family. The copies are homeomorphic to M but not similar: M is quasi-self-similar, not self-similar, and the copies' hairs and decorations differ.
- **Universality.** The same structure (cardioid, bulbs, baby copies) appears in the parameter spaces of very different one-parameter families (cubic slices, Newton's method, exponential maps), because renormalisation reduces them locally to the quadratic case. The Mandelbrot set is universal in the sense of Feigenbaum's constants being universal.
- **MLC.** The central open problem: is M locally connected? If so, Douady–Hubbard's combinatorial model (the "pinched disc" obtained by identifying ray landing points) describes M completely and hyperbolicity follows. Yoccoz proved local connectivity at all parameters that are not infinitely renormalisable; Lyubich and others extended it to many infinitely renormalisable parameters; the general case remains open.
- **Boundary dimension.** Shishikura (1998) proved the boundary of M has Hausdorff dimension 2, the maximum possible for a plane curve: as rough as a boundary can be.
- **Hubbard's style.** The lecture is mathematics, not metaphor: definitions, theorems, pictures generated by computation, and open problems, with emphasis that computer pictures suggest theorems that then require proof.

## What Mandel adopts

- **Keep parameter space and state space distinct.** Mandel's "state" is a market or a strategy's current condition; its "parameter space" is the catalogue of model and policy settings. Most confusion in trading systems comes from mixing them; M is the model of keeping them apart.
- **A catalogue of validated regions, not a single point.** Hyperbolic components are regions of parameter space with stable behaviour; Mandel's archive should record *regions* of settings that passed the gate (with their boundaries), not just the single best setting, because near a component boundary behaviour changes qualitatively.
- **Renormalisation as the honest form of self-similarity.** Baby copies are homeomorphic, not identical, and appear after re-scaling a suitable iterate. Mandel's "same loop at several scales" (ORIGINAL-IDEAS #7) should expect the same *shape* of procedure with different decorations, and must re-derive, not copy, the budgets and thresholds at each scale.
- **External rays as provenance.** A point of M is identified by the angles of the rays that land on it; Mandel identifies a decision by the chain of evidence that lands on it (the ledger), and two decisions with the same provenance pattern are "the same" even if their surface looks different.
- **Pictures suggest theorems; proofs or tests decide.** The signal map and backtest plots are hypotheses generators; the gate is the proof step.
- **Roughness has a ceiling.** Boundary dimension 2 is the maximum; in Mandel's domain, the analogue is that some boundaries (regime changes, resolution events) are as unpredictable as they can be, and the model should say so rather than fit them.

## What Mandel holds loosely or rejects

- Any claim that markets *are* a Mandelbrot set or that z² + c predicts prices. The transferable content is the method (parameter space, stability regions, renormalisation), not the map.
- Visual similarity between M's structures and market charts: Tan Lei similarity is a theorem about a specific holomorphic family; charts are not holomorphic dynamics.
- Treating "universality" as a licence to transfer settings between children or markets without a transfer test (A1's ADAS lesson applies).

## Design implications for the RSI predictive substrate

- `ProposalBook` niches become *regions*: each accepted proposal records the parameter ranges over which the gate held (from the walk-forward windows), and a `region_map` query returns, for a target, the known stable ranges and the gaps.
- A "boundary flag" in the gate: if a candidate's parameters sit within one grid step of a known failure, require an extra out-of-sample window (behaviour near component boundaries is where small moves change everything).
- The signal map gains a parameter-space view: for a chosen strategy, settings tried so far coloured by gate outcome, with accepted regions outlined.
- Renormalisation discipline for ORIGINAL-IDEAS #7: each scale's cycle has its own written budget and thresholds derived from measured cost and resolution rate at that scale, with the derivation logged.
- Metrics: number of validated regions per target, fraction of proposals landing near a boundary, and how often a boundary flag changed the outcome.
